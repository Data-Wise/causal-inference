This document provides a detailed timeline for your final project. The goal is to break the project into manageable steps and ensure you are making steady progress. Following this schedule will help you produce a more thoughtful and rigorous final product.

Summary of Major Deadlines

Deliverable Due Date Weight
Project Proposal End of Week 9 10%
Final Presentation In Class, Week 14 10%
Final Paper End of Finals Week 25%

Using Canvas for Project Deliverables 💻

We will use Canvas to facilitate feedback and track progress throughout the semester. In general, there are two types of deliverables:

  • Discussion Boards (Peer Feedback): For early-stage brainstorming and feedback (e.g., sharing your DAG), you will post your work to a weekly discussion board. The goal is to learn from each other and refine your ideas. Participation requires posting your own work and providing constructive feedback to at least one classmate.
  • Assignments (Check-ins & Graded Submissions): For formal submissions, you will upload a document to a Canvas Assignment. Some of these will be ungraded check-ins to ensure you are on track (e.g., submitting a diagnostic plot), while the three major deadlines will be graded.

Part I: Foundations (Weeks 1-4)

The focus of this part is on defining a strong, feasible causal question and building the theoretical foundation for your analysis.

Week 1: The Causal Question

  • Project Focus: Brainstorming and selecting a topic.
  • Connection to Course: We are defining what a causal effect is. Your task is to find one you want to estimate.
  • Suggested Tasks:
    • Explore the Data Resources page and other sources.
    • Do a quick literature search to see if your potential questions are novel and feasible.
  • Deliverable: Post 2-3 potential causal questions to the “Week 1: Project Brainstorming” discussion board on Canvas.

Week 2: The DAG

  • Project Focus: Specifying your causal assumptions.
  • Connection to Course: We are learning the language of DAGs. You will apply it directly to your research question.
  • Suggested Tasks:
    • Settle on a single causal question from your Week 1 list.
    • Draw a preliminary DAG including your exposure, outcome, and key potential confounders.
    • Write a short paragraph justifying the nodes and arrows.
  • Deliverable: Post an image of your DAG and your justification paragraph to the “Week 2: DAG Peer Feedback” discussion board. Provide thoughtful feedback on at least one classmate’s DAG.

Week 3: Assumptions

  • Project Focus: Articulating your identification strategy.
  • Connection to Course: We’re formalizing exchangeability, positivity, and consistency.
  • Suggested Tasks:
    • Using your DAG, identify a sufficient adjustment set.
    • Consider if these variables are available in your data. Note any critical unmeasured confounders.
  • Deliverable: In a text entry, submit your identified adjustment set and a 1-2 sentence statement on the plausibility of positivity to the “Week 3: Assumptions Check-in” assignment (ungraded).

Week 4: Study Design

  • Project Focus: Emulating the Target Trial.
  • Connection to Course: We are discussing sources of bias. The target trial framework helps us proactively minimize them.
  • Suggested Tasks:
    • Begin drafting the protocol for your hypothetical target trial.
    • Focus on Eligibility Criteria, Treatment Strategies, and Assignment Procedure.
  • Deliverable: Post a 1-paragraph summary of your target trial’s eligibility criteria to the “Week 4: Target Trial Design” discussion board.

Part II: Estimation (Weeks 5-9)

The focus now shifts from theory to practice. You will begin implementing methods to estimate your causal effect.

Week 5: Propensity Scores

  • Project Focus: Modeling the exposure.
  • Connection to Course: We are learning about propensity scores (PS). You will now estimate them.
  • Suggested Tasks:
    • Write the code to fit your PS model.
    • Generate diagnostic plots (e.g., histograms or density plots) to check for overlap.
  • Deliverable: Submit a PDF of your PS overlap plot to the “Week 5: PS Overlap Check-in” assignment (ungraded).

Weeks 6 & 7: Weighting & G-Computation

  • Project Focus: Estimating the causal effect.
  • Connection to Course: We are covering IPTW and G-computation.
  • Suggested Tasks:
    • Calculate inverse probability of treatment weights (IPTW).
    • Check for covariate balance after weighting using a Love plot from the cobalt package.
    • Use the weights to get a preliminary estimate of the average treatment effect.
  • Deliverable: Submit a PDF of your covariate balance (Love) plot to the “Week 7: Covariate Balance Check-in” assignment (ungraded).

Week 8: Instrumental Variables

  • Project Focus: Project proposal refinement.
  • Connection to Course: Understanding IV helps you think critically about confounding.
  • Suggested Tasks:
    • Consolidate your causal question, DAG, target trial protocol, and proposed methods into a single document.
    • Draft all sections of the proposal.
  • Deliverable: This is a dedicated work week. No deliverable is due as you prepare the proposal.

Week 9: Effect Modification

  • Project Focus: Proposal Submission.
  • Connection to Course: Thinking about heterogeneity may inform your project’s secondary aims.
  • Suggested Tasks:
    • Finalize and polish your proposal document.
  • Deliverable: Submit your final Project Proposal PDF to the “Project Proposal” assignment on Canvas (Graded).

Part III: Refinement & Reporting (Weeks 10-14)

The final part is about ensuring your results are robust, interpreting them correctly, and communicating them effectively.

Weeks 10 & 11: Mediation

  • Project Focus: Writing the Methods and Results sections.
  • Connection to Course: Thinking about mediation helps refine your causal pathways.
  • Suggested Tasks:
    • Write a full draft of your paper’s Methods and Results sections.
    • Create your “Table 1” and all figures.
  • Deliverable: This is a dedicated work week for writing. No deliverable is due.

Week 12: Sensitivity Analysis

  • Project Focus: Assessing robustness.
  • Connection to Course: Direct application of sensitivity analysis techniques.
  • Suggested Tasks:
    • Calculate an E-value for your primary finding.
    • Write up the sensitivity analysis portion of your paper.
  • Deliverable: Post your main effect estimate and its corresponding E-value, along with a 1-sentence interpretation, to the “Week 12: E-value Check-in” discussion board.

Week 13: Target Trial Emulation

  • Project Focus: Finalizing the paper and presentation.
  • Connection to Course: A synthesis of the target trial framework.
  • Suggested Tasks:
    • Write the Introduction and Discussion sections of your paper.
    • Create the slides for your final presentation and practice your timing.
  • Deliverable: Submit your presentation slides (PDF or PPTX) to the “Week 13: Presentation Slides” assignment (ungraded) so they are ready for next week.

Week 14: Final Presentations

  • Project Focus: Communicating your research.
  • Deliverable: In-class project presentations (Graded).

Finals Week

  • Project Focus: Final revisions.
  • Suggested Tasks:
    • Incorporate feedback from your presentation.
    • Proofread and format your paper carefully.
  • Deliverable: Submit your Final Paper PDF to the “Final Paper” assignment on Canvas (Graded).
