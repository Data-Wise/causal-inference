Schedule at a Glance

  • Week 1 — Instructor demo (no submissions)

  • Week 2 — DAGs & Assumption Transparency Pearl (1995) Biometrika. Focus: d‑separation, backdoor sets, DAG as data‑generating story.

  • Week 3 — Exchangeability, Positivity, Consistency Rubin (1974) JEP. Focus: \(Y^1,Y^0\), SUTVA, exchangeability vs. design.

  • Week 4 — Confounding, Selection, Measurement Bias Hernán, Hernández‑Díaz, & Robins (2004) Epidemiology. Focus: collider/selection bias, loss to follow‑up as conditioning.

  • Week 5 — Propensity Scores Rosenbaum & Rubin (1983) Biometrika. Focus: balancing score, overlap, matching/stratification/weighting.

  • Week 6 — IPTW, MSMs, DR Robins, Hernán, & Brumback (2000) Epidemiology. Focus: time‑varying confounding, stabilized weights, pseudo‑populations.

  • Week 7 — G‑computation Snowden, Rose, & Mortimer (2011) AJE. Focus: standardization via outcome modeling vs. IPTW.

  • Week 8 — Instrumental Variables Angrist, Imbens, & Rubin (1996) JASA. Focus: IV assumptions, LATE, monotonicity, principal strata.

  • Week 9 — Effect Modification Yusuf et al. (1991) JAMA. Focus: pre‑specification, multiplicity, tests of interaction.

  • Week 10 — Mediation I (Foundations) Pearl (2014) Psychological Methods. Focus: CDE vs. NDE/NIE, mediation formulae and assumptions.

  • Week 11 — Mediation II & Principal Stratification Imai, Keele, & Tingley (2010) Psychological Methods. Focus: sequential ignorability, software, sensitivity.

  • Week 12 — Sensitivity Analysis VanderWeele & Ding (2017) Ann Intern Med. Focus: E‑value interpretation/limits.

  • Week 13 — Target Trial Emulation Hernán & Robins (2016) AJE. Focus: 7 components, time zero, immortal time, assignment procedure.

Critique Deliverables & Deadlines

  • What to submit each week (Weeks 2–13):
    • 2‑page critique (max 900 words, not counting figures/refs)
    • One-page DAG with minimal sufficient adjustment set(s) highlighted
    • Assumption log (bullet list) + robustness plan (≤10 bullets)
    • Reproducible appendix (R code or pseudocode) for at least one key diagnostic (balance, overlap, weight diagnostics, or a small sensitivity calc)
  • When: Mondays 11:59 pm MT (the night before our Tue class)
  • Where: Canvas → “Weekly Paper Critique — Week X”
  • Collaboration: Discuss ideas freely; write/code your own submission.

How to Write a Great Critique

Use our Assumption‑Centric 4‑Step:

  • Study Design → Causal Question

    • State the estimand (e.g., \(ATE\), \(ATT\), \(LATE\), NDE/NIE).
    • Write the target trial version in one paragraph (eligibility, treatment strategies, time zero, outcome, follow‑up, causal contrast).

  • Method Selection → Identification

    • Draw the DAG; list minimal backdoor sets (if applicable).
    • If IV/mediation/MSM, write assumptions explicitly (relevance, independence, exclusion; sequential ignorability; positivity; consistency; no interference).
    • Note any positivity risks (sparse cells/extreme propensities).

  • Assumption Validation → Diagnostics

    • Exchangeability: covariate balance & overlap plots; placebo/negative controls (if available).
    • Positivity: common support figures, weight distribution summaries (mean/sd/skew, max).
    • Modeling: mis‑specification checks, flexible learners where helpful.
    • Sensitivity: E‑values; IV falsification tests; Rosenbaum bounds; alternative specs.

  • Interpretation → Uncertainty Communication

    • Interpret the causal estimand with audience‑appropriate language; flag scope (population, time).
    • State where violations would flip conclusions.
    • Offer at least one alternative method that could triangulate the main claim.

Templates & Checklists

A) 2‑Page Critique Template (copy/paste)

  • Title: Week X — [Paper Title] — STAT 579 Critique
  • Causal Question & Estimand (≤120 words): …
  • Target Trial (≤120 words): Eligibility; treatment strategies; time zero; outcome; follow‑up; causal contrast.
  • DAG (figure): include nodes and arrows; highlight chosen adjustment set.
  • Identification Plan (≤150 words): backdoor/IV/mediation/MSM rationale.
  • Diagnostics (bullets): balance/overlap; weight quality; model checks.
  • Sensitivity (bullets): E‑value or alternative; what violation would overturn results.
  • Interpretation (≤120 words): estimand in plain language; limitations; - generalizability.
  • Repro Appendix: code or pseudocode for one diagnostic.

B) DAG Checklist (quick yes/no)

C) Positivity & Weight Quality

  • Report: % with \(\hat e(X)\in[0.1,0.9]\); histogram/density; stabilized weight mean≈1, sd small, max reasonable (<10 suggested for weekly demos).
  • If violations: restrict support, coarsen covariates, or switch method (e.g., g‑comp/TMLE).

D) Sensitivity Menu (pick ≥1)

  • E‑value for point & CI limit
  • Rosenbaum bounds (binary outcomes)
  • Placebo/negative control (if plausible)
  • IV falsification (balance on observables; over‑id tests with caution)
  • Sequential ignorability stress tests (mediation)

Starter R Snippets

Propensity, Overlap, and Balance

library(WeightIt); library(cobalt)
w <- weightit(A ~ X1+X2+X3, data=df, method="ps", estimand="ATE")
love.plot(bal.tab(w), thresholds=c(m=.1))
summary(w$weights)  # check mean≈1 (if stabilized), max, skew

IPTW for MSM (stabilized)

num <- glm(A ~ L0, family=binomial, data=df)      # numerator model (coarse)
den <- glm(A ~ L0+L1, family=binomial, data=df)   # denominator model
sw  <- predict(num, type="response")/predict(den, type="response")
msm <- glm(Y ~ A, weights=sw, data=df)            # simple MSM

G‑computation (parametric)

mY <- glm(Y ~ A + X1 + X2, family=gaussian, data=df)
mu1 <- mean(predict(mY, newdata=transform(df, A=1), type="response"))
mu0 <- mean(predict(mY, newdata=transform(df, A=0), type="response"))
ATE <- mu1 - mu0

Mediation (NDE/NIE via mediation)

library(mediation)
mM <- glm(M ~ A + X, family=binomial, data=df)
mY <- glm(Y ~ A + M + X, family=binomial, data=df)
med <- mediate(mM, mY, treat="A", mediator="M", sims=1000)
summary(med)  # NDE/NIE under sequential ignorability

E‑value (risk ratio)

library(EValue)
evalue.RR(est=2.5, lo=1.8, hi=3.4)

Evaluation Rubric

Dimension Points What “Excellent” Looks Like
Causal question & estimand clarity 10 Precise estimand; clean target‑trial description
DAG & identification 25 Correct minimal set; no collider adjustment; right method for the structure
Diagnostics 20 Balance, overlap, weight quality, model checks shown clearly
Sensitivity 15 At least one appropriate sensitivity tool; interprets what strength is needed to overturn
Interpretation & uncertainty 15 Plain‑language, scope noted; limitations tied to assumptions
Reproducibility appendix 10 Runs or is plausibly runnable; shows key diagnostic
Writing & figures 5 Clear, concise, well‑labeled

Late policy: −10% per day (course policy).

Helpful Resources

  • Open textbooks (free):
    • Hernán & Robins, What If — target trials, MSM/IPTW, g‑methods
    • Neal, Introduction to Causal Inference — DAGs, backdoor, IPW/DR
    • Pearl, Glymour, & Jewell, Causal Inference in Statistics: A Primer — backdoor/frontdoor, SCM intuition
    • VanderWeele, Explanation in Causal Inference — mediation & interaction (foundational)
    • Wager, STATS 361 Notes — DR/AIPW, IV/LATE, heterogeneity
  • R packages: dagitty, ggdag, MatchIt, WeightIt, cobalt, ipw, twang, ivreg, mediation, EValue, tmle3
  • Diagnostic expectations per topic:
    • PS/Matching (Week 5): SMDs < 0.1; overlap plots; matched sample description
    • MSM/IPTW (Week 6): stabilized weight moments; truncation sensitivity
    • G‑comp (Week 7): flexible outcome model; residual checks; alt link funcs
    • IV (Week 8): 3 assumptions stated; first‑stage strength; monotonicity rationale; LATE interpretation
    • Mediation (Weeks 10–11): explicit sequential ignorability; cross‑world cautions; sensitivity param(s)
    • Sensitivity (Week 12): E‑value prose for non‑statisticians; limits acknowledged
    • Target trial (Week 13): 7 components table; immortal time avoidance; assignment emulation plan

Submission Logistics

  • File names: Wk##_Lastname_Firstname.pdf (+ optional .R or .qmd)
  • Figures: readable in grayscale; include captions; axes labeled
  • Reproducibility: seed set; simulated data OK but mark clearly; no PHI/sensitive data
